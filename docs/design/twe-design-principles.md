# TWE Design Principles

Estimating tax withholding is inherently complex. We take on that complexity so taxpayers don't have to. These design principles guide us in creating an experience that supports accurate estimates, inspires confidence, and builds trust with taxpayers.

## Give taxpayers control over their level of accuracy

Some taxpayers want a quick estimate while others want a more detailed estimate. Support different goals and levels of effort by giving taxpayers flexibility in how much information they provide and how they can tailor their withholding. Let taxpayers provide optional information to improve accuracy when they choose.

## Protect taxpayers from underwithholding

Help taxpayers avoid unexpected tax bills and potential underwithholding penalties by supporting more predictable tax outcomes.

* Make it easy to report all sources of income accurately.
* Make it easy to identify and transfer the right data from income documents into the estimator.
* Support anticipated life and income changes by allowing taxpayers to enter future income.
* Consider the underwithholding risk when choosing what to collect.

## Reduce cognitive load

Reduce overwhelm and the mental effort required to complete the estimator. Guide taxpayers through the process with a clear, predictable experience that makes complex information easier to understand.

* Guide taxpayers through data collection one step at a time.
* Organize content and sequence questions to match taxpayers’ mental models.
* Use simple, accessible design patterns aligned with the U.S. Web Design System.
* Reveal complex information progressively and provide contextual help (e.g., links, modals) when it's needed.

## Less is more

Every question in the estimator has the potential to confuse taxpayers due to the complexity of taxes, diversity of individual tax situations, and range of tax literacy.

Design for confidence and reduce complexity wherever possible:

* Ask only the questions needed for an accurate estimate.
* Use plain language so taxpayers can answer with confidence.
* Organize questions in a clear, logical sequence that feels natural, intuitive, and easy to follow.

## Provide transparency

Making something easy to use doesn’t necessarily mean hiding all the complexity. If we hide too much, that can obscure withholding logic and increase anxiety. We aim to build trust by providing just enough transparency to help taxpayers understand what’s going on without overwhelming them so they feel empowered to make informed decisions.

* Find ways to minimize discomfort when asking sensitive questions and explain why this information is needed.
* Show the math.
* Reveal additional details when taxpayers want to learn more.

## Anticipate errors so they're easy to fix

Taxpayers are human, have varying levels of financial and tax knowledge, and make mistakes. These mistakes can impact estimation accuracy and cause confusion. Design for mistakes by helping taxpayers avoid errors, recognize them quickly, and recover with confidence. A resilient experience improves both accuracy and trust.

* Provide context to support informed decisions.
* Help taxpayers identify, understand, and resolve errors.
* Prevent dead-ends and make recovery paths clear.
* Use thoughtful "speed bumps" to encourage careful review of complex, tricky, or high-impact questions.

## Provide clear expectations and actionable results

Respect taxpayers' time by making the process predictable, easy to navigate, and focused on meaningful outcomes. Help taxpayers understand what they need, what to expect, and what actions to take next.

* Clearly communicate required documents and information before getting started.
* Guide taxpayers through each step of the estimation process.
* Use clear labels and language to help taxpayers focus only on relevant information.
* Provide actionable recommendations for adjusting withholding and avoiding unexpected tax bills or penalties.

## Build tax literacy and empower taxpayers

Tax literacy is an equity issue—when taxpayers don't understand, they're more likely to make mistakes, go without income from their paycheck, or face a surprise tax payment at the end of the year.

Improved tax literacy leads to better decisions, fewer errors, and more confidence throughout the process.

* Explain tax concepts in plain language.
* Provide the context needed to support informed decision-making.
* Use progressive disclosure to introduce complex information when it’s relevant.
* Design for varying levels of tax knowledge and changing life and income circumstances.

## Taxpayers are real people

Design and content decisions should be informed by what best serves taxpayers: an incredibly diverse group with an array of life circumstances, financial situations, and levels of tax knowledge. The estimator needs to be accessible to taxpayers who have a variety of attitudes, aptitudes, abilities, and access needs.

Good design is design that works for everyone.

* Offer the tool in English and Spanish.
* Meet (and exceed) accessibility standards.
* Reduce taxpayer’s cognitive burden in the face of stressful life and financial changes.
* Exclusively design in mobile dimensions.
